Model and limits

Web Audio synthesizes continuous engine and road sound at the active observer. The strongest 32 sources have individual engine voices; remaining modeled traffic contributes to the ambient mix. This perceptual audio mix is not the phase-resolved comparison solver. Audio starts off. Synthetic V-twin engines use uneven firing pulses, low exhaust resonance, and throttle-linked texture rather than recordings. Nearby engines dominate the playback mix, with extra perceptual attenuation farther away and a quiet ambient bed. The synthetic high-noise scenario uses 136 dB(Z) motorcycle sources at 1 m and a 20 dBA background; cars remain independent at 74 dB(Z). Estimated dBA still depends on engine spectrum, motion, source count, geometry, and propagation, not fixed distance bands. A compressor limits the combined mix. Volume never changes the dBA calculation.

NYC street and building geometry; synthetic traffic demand, signals, source levels, and engine waveforms. No field calibration.

The live map includes every simulated source with travel delay, distance loss and approximate roof diffraction. First-order facade and treatment paths are resolved for nearby sources; farther sources retain direct and diffracted energy. Traffic paths use incoherent energy. Marked cancellation nodes add an output-limited narrowband phase estimate with controller delay; this is not full broadband cancellation. Detailed comparisons use the separate waveform solver.

Colors show a 7-by-7 outdoor sample grid near street height over the viewed area. The active microphone and observation markers are measured separately at their actual heights. The timestamp identifies the latest completed sample; visual movement does not imply a fresh acoustic result.
