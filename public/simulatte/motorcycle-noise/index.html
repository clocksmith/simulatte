Traffic-source energy budget

Cumulative packet accounting from the start of the pass. Bypassing energy and energy traveling toward the panel remain explicit.

Powered contribution

Active cancellation adds energy; it is not passive redirection. Its pressure can reduce sound locally and increase it elsewhere. Ambient noise is outside the traffic-source packet budget.

Model and limits

NYC street and building geometry; synthetic traffic and sound. These results are not field measurements.

  • Vehicles use smoothed street paths, acceleration, headway, and modeled junction signals. Pedestrians wait at modeled crossings. Lane direction, counts, and signal timing are assumptions.
  • Acoustics includes finite travel time, up to twelve nearby first-order facade candidates, and approximate roof diffraction. Facade pressure reflection is assumed to be 0.55.
  • Source waveforms include firing harmonics and synthetic broadband noise. The 8 kHz solver omits frequencies above 4 kHz; it is not full-band traffic acoustics.
  • Surface reflection, transmission, and absorption are separate from powered cancellation. The returning field follows emission geometry and may miss a moving motorcycle.
  • The energy ledger covers source emissions and direct panel interception, not a complete canyon energy balance. Active output is accounted for separately.
  • Street samples are 12 m apart. Heatmap colors interpolate acoustic energy between outdoor samples; they do not resolve every interference fringe. Local listener samples are 0.25 m apart.
  • No guaranteed decibel reduction, rider identification, calibrated traffic, or manufactured reflector performance is claimed.
