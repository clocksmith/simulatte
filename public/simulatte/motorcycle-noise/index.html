Model and limits

Web Audio synthesizes continuous engine and road sound at the active observer. The strongest 32 sources have individual engine voices; remaining modeled traffic contributes to the ambient mix. This perceptual audio mix is not the phase-resolved comparison solver. Audio starts off. Synthetic V-twin engines use uneven firing pulses, low exhaust resonance, and throttle-linked texture rather than recordings. Playback uses the calculated viewpoint contributions without a second distance penalty. The strongest 32 voices represent the traffic mix. A compressed listening range targets -28 dBFS at 80 modeled dBA, -23 dBFS at 90, and -13 dBFS at 110, before output processing. The playback meter measures digital output, not physical speaker SPL. Device volume, headphones, and speakers are uncalibrated. Background ambience remains quiet. The synthetic high-noise scenario uses a 136 dB(Z) full-load motorcycle reference at 1 m, with modeled idle at 86 dB(Z) and steady cruising up to 114 dB(Z) and a 20 dBA background; cars remain independent at 74 dB(Z). Estimated dBA still depends on engine spectrum, motion, source count, geometry, and propagation, not fixed distance bands. A compressor limits the combined mix. Device volume never changes the dBA calculation.
