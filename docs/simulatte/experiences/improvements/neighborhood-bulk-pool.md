## Frontier improvements

Neighborhood Bulk Pool reaches 10/10 when it feels like a living cooperative
marketplace, not a generic vehicle-routing demonstration, and every package,
dollar, handoff, and privacy decision remains understandable.

The experience starts with household requests across Manhattan, Brooklyn,
and Queens, using a warehouse-scale catalog from East Harlem, Sunset Park, Long
Island City, and Rego Park. Users search any governed product,
request fractional quantities, specify substitutions, freshness constraints,
pickup radius, availability window, and maximum price. Drivers publish existing
trip intent, spare capacity, refrigeration, detour tolerance, and compensation
mode: volunteer, exact-cost reimbursement, or disclosed fee.

The simulation visibly progresses through demand aggregation, package
formation, warehouse selection, driver matching, purchase, splitting, routing,
handoffs, failures, and settlement. Selecting a package reveals who
requested each share without exposing identities or exact addresses. Selecting
a vehicle explains its original trip, added distance, cargo, temperature
obligations, compensation, and remaining capacity.

Interest comes from recognizable tradeoffs and recoverable disruptions: an
item goes out of stock, weighted produce costs more than estimated, a driver
cancels, frozen capacity is exceeded, a bridge delay threatens freshness, or a
household rejects a substitution. Users can intervene and watch
the pool repair itself. Compare mode synchronizes independent shopping,
pooled purchasing, existing-trip delivery, and neighborhood pickup hubs.

Consistency requires the shared playback, camera, comparison, metrics, receipt,
and settlement contracts. Every control causally affects allocation or
routing. Inventory conserves whole packages and fractional shares; costs
reconcile exactly to receipts, taxes, discounts, deposits, refunds,
mileage, tolls, and fees. Synthetic demand never appears to represent real
residents, while stale warehouse observations are clearly timestamped.

A 10/10 user immediately understands what was purchased, how it was divided,
why each driver was selected, how much everyone saved, where waste remained,
and whether any participant carried an unfair burden.

## Acceptance gates

- [ ] Desktop and mobile browser evidence proves catalog filtering and fractional
      shares remain legible without implying complete live inventory.
- [ ] Visual playback proves whole-package splitting, vehicle capacity, and cold
      custody using the same run identity as the terminal receipt.
- [ ] A public mid-run control can apply stockout, cancellation, and detour actions
      and visibly show the recomputed plan.
- [ ] Participant settlement visibly reconciles base scenario price, unitemized
      expense reimbursement, and disclosed fees without invented tax or toll splits.
- [ ] Privacy review confirms no direct household address or real-person identity
      enters state, labels, inspections, receipts, or copied reports.
- [ ] Synchronized policy comparison, seek, replay, reload, and terminal settlement
      reproduce the same scenario identity and conserved totals.
