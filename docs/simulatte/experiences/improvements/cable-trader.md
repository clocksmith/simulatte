## Frontier improvements

Cable Trader reaches 10/10 when the exchange feels continuously inhabited while
the supply and demand board remains readable. The core product is no longer a
crisis or policy comparison. It is a practical neighborhood alternative to
ordering a missing cable: a person checks a hub, picks up the cable, and later
another person returns a spare.

The default 256-person scale renders one tiny node per unique residence
without implying that all people travel at once. The map keeps the bounded live
traveler sample honest, removes flashing traveler labels, and leaves detailed
cable identity in inspection. The inspector keeps global totals and every hub's
local balance available without crowding the map or hiding the three controls
that materially rebuild the pseudo-year.

The experience should also make mismatches understandable. A hub can hold spare
HDMI while people wait elsewhere for USB-C, so inventory and waiting demand may
coexist globally. Selecting a person should connect that journey to the named
cable, action, hub, residence, and route. The board, map, progressive state, and
receipts must continue to describe the same daily snapshot.

A 10/10 user can explain how many people and unique residences participate, which hubs
are active, what cables are being offered and requested, who is currently
carrying a cable, and how many purchases the reuse network avoided during the
modeled year.
