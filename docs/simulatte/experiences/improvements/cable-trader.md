## Frontier improvements

Cable Trader reaches 10/10 when the exchange feels continuously inhabited while
the supply and demand board remains readable. The core product is no longer a
crisis or policy comparison. It is a practical neighborhood alternative to
ordering a missing cable: a person checks a hub, picks up the cable, and later
another person returns a spare.

The next review should determine whether the default 6,000-person scale is
legible without implying that all people travel at once. The map must make the
bounded live sample honest, keep pickup and drop-off direction recognizable,
and preserve cable identity through tone and labels. The inspector must make
global totals and each hub's local balance scannable without hiding the four
controls that materially rebuild the pseudo-year.

The experience should also make mismatches understandable. A hub can hold spare
HDMI while people wait elsewhere for USB-C, so inventory and waiting demand may
coexist globally. Selecting a person should connect that journey to the named
cable, action, hub, location, and route. The board, map, progressive state, and
receipts must continue to describe the same daily snapshot.

A 10/10 user can explain how many people participate, which hubs and locations
are active, what cables are being offered and requested, who is currently
carrying a cable, and how many purchases the reuse network avoided during the
modeled year.

## Acceptance gates

- [ ] Current browser evidence confirms that people, cable type, action, hub,
      and location are immediately recognizable.
- [ ] Global supply and demand visibly reconcile to the active hub boards.
- [ ] People, hubs, locations, and cable set controls visibly rebuild the run.
- [ ] Desktop and mobile layouts keep the live board and map readable.
- [ ] User review confirms that the experience reads as continuous cable reuse,
      not restoration, crisis response, or an abstract allocation dashboard.
