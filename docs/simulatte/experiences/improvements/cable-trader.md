## Frontier improvements

Cable Trader reaches 10/10 when it stops feeling like an allocation dashboard
and becomes a visible, consequential logistics story whose numbers and
animation always describe the same run.

The user should begin with a concrete crisis: several neighborhoods or projects
need specific cable families, but available reels differ in length, conductor
type, compatibility, location, cost, and delivery time. Controls must expose the
meaningful decisions: demand priority, acceptable substitutes, reserve policy,
transfer capacity, allocation objective, fairness weighting, and disruption
assumptions. Every change must rebuild the allocation and visibly alter
shortages, transfers, cost, and completion outcomes.

The world should show individual depots, demand sites, reel inventory, and
moving transfers. Cable must remain distinguishable by family and quantity
throughout the simulation. A reel cannot silently become generic capacity. When
cable leaves a depot, its inventory should decrease; when it arrives, the
receiving project should visibly progress. Partial reels, unusable remnants,
compatibility failures, delayed vehicles, and contested allocations should all
be represented. Selecting any transfer should explain its origin, destination,
quantity, reason, alternatives rejected, and downstream consequence.

Interest comes from decisions with understandable tension. The experience
should include staged disruptions such as urgent repairs, road closures,
damaged stock, surprise demand, or a fairness conflict, and let users intervene
while playback continues. Compare mode should synchronize at least three
policies: cheapest allocation, fastest restoration, and fairness-first
allocation. The difference should be obvious on the map before reading metrics.

Consistency requires one shared vocabulary and control layout, the standard
camera modes, deterministic replay, timeline scrubbing, side metrics,
settlement receipts, and disabled controls when unavailable. No decorative
action button should complete instantly without visible state progression.

A 10/10 user can explain what moved, why it moved, who benefited, who remained
short, what policy caused that result, and what changed between comparison
branches, using both the animation and matching receipts.
